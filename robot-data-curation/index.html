What robotics labs actually do to their pretraining data

Every recent robot foundation model claims to scale. Far fewer say what they threw away, what they kept, and why. This is a stage-by-stage reading of the data curation pipelines behind 47 works released since 2025 — including the ones that publish nothing at all.

Cross-cutting

Three answers to the same question

Every pipeline here confronts mixed-quality data. They resolve it in one of three ways, and the choice determines how far the corpus can grow.

Filter it out

Reject before training

A cascade of checks removes what cannot serve the objective. Effective, and expensive — you pay for every stage, and you lose coverage you cannot get back.

  • Qwen-RobotManip — 5 stages, 81% of one subset
  • Cosmos 3 — 9.8% image yield
  • Hydra-0 — 16.7% of windows
  • ABot-World-0 — 14 checks, 6 dimensions
Label it and condition

Keep it, but tell the model

Quality becomes an input feature rather than a rejection decision, so the corpus keeps growing past the point where filtering would have capped it.

  • π₀.₇ — quality 1–5, mistake flags, speed
  • π*₀.₆ / RECAP — learned advantage
  • AGIBOT — annotated error-recovery
  • ACE-Brain-0.5 — offline deviation metadata
Route it elsewhere

Send rejects to a weaker objective

What fails one quality bar still trains a less demanding loss. In DYNA-2's ablation, that discarded tier is where cross-embodiment transfer actually comes from.

  • DYNA-2 — video-prediction tier
  • ACE-Ego-0 — reliability-weighted aux. loss
  • ABot-World-0 — soft flags → sample weights
  • Cosmos 3 — minor artifacts flagged, kept
Quality filtering is only the first layer. A visually clean clip is not necessarily control-informative. Kairos, §4.2 — the sharpest reframing in the survey

01 The objective sets the curation bill

How much cleaning you must do is decided by what you train on the data, not by the data itself. Being-H0.7's latent-future alignment needs only temporal coherence, so it absorbs 200,000 hours. Video prediction needs generable pixels. Pseudo-action labelling needs accurate hand tracking, and is the most expensive of the three.

DreamZero states it outright: a world-modelling objective "enables effective learning from diverse demonstrations," which licensed them to collect messy, non-repetitive data that an action-only policy could not have used.

02 Alignment is a precondition for scaling

Qwen-RobotManip's ablation is the cleanest result in the set: with a unified end-effector representation, validation loss falls log-linearly with data across the full range. Without it, the same data exhibits no scaling behaviour at all.

π₀.₇ reports the mirror image from the quality side — with metadata conditioning, performance keeps improving as average data quality falls; without it, adding lower-quality data makes the model worse.

03 A small aligned anchor unlocks a large noisy corpus

Four works reach this independently. EgoScale mixes 829 h of clean EgoDex into 20,854 h of wild video. EgoVerse finds 2 h of domain-aligned data unlocks transfer from 8 h of otherwise inert diverse data. Galaxea inserts a single-embodiment bridge stage. Ψ₀ post-trains on 30 h of robot data.

The convergence is strong enough to read as a design rule: diverse data is inert until something grounds it.

04 A filter is also a distribution shift

Only Cosmos 3 measures this. Analysing retention by capability category, they find pruning becomes strongly category-selective as thresholds tighten — referring-expression grounding is culled hardest, captioning and VQA decline substantially, OCR survives. Hence deliberately conservative thresholds, "minimizing excessive distribution shift across capability domains."

Everyone else raises a threshold and reports the resulting corpus, not the capability they deleted.

05 Diversity beats density past a threshold

EgoVerse: once quantity is moderate, scene coverage gains where within-scene density does not. DreamZero: diverse data beats repetitive data at matched hours. AXIS beats a volume-matched RoboCasa365 baseline by 37.3%. GEN-0, holder of the largest corpus here, concludes that "data quality and diversity matters more than sheer volume."

All four are hour-matched or volume-matched comparisons, which is what makes them worth anything.

06 Success-only corpora cannot teach recovery

Kairos names the categories the field is missing: near-boundary failures, recovery events, near-boundary successes, contact transitions, safety anomalies, long-horizon dependencies. Nobody else names the third — the clips where things almost went wrong and didn't are where safety margins live.

AGIBOT retains and annotates error-recovery trajectories; π₀.₇ labels mistakes; RECAP grades autonomous rollouts by learned advantage. Kairos supplies the theory for all three, and concedes its own detector is not built yet.

07 Order your filters by cost

The pipelines that scale put cheap deterministic rejection first. ACE-Ego-0 runs face detection and caption keyword checks before HaMeR reconstruction. ABot-World-0's Stage 1 is format validation, "enabling rapid rejection before computationally intensive analyses." Cosmos 3 designs around the fact that the pipeline is low-yield and highly iterative, so wasted inference dominates the bill.

08 Disclosure does not track capability

The works publishing numeric thresholds are Alibaba (three separate teams), NVIDIA, ACE, and Kairos. The works publishing essentially nothing are Google DeepMind, Figure, Skild, 1X, AGIBOT, and mimic.

Gemini Robotics 2's model card resolves its entire data section to the Gemini 3.5 Flash card. GR00T N1.7 — the baseline half the field benchmarks against — has had no data disclosure since N1. Comparative claims about curation practice simply cannot include the closed frontier.

Unresolved

Volume or purity? The field has not run the experiment

Three works make incompatible claims about the same substrate. Each is internally well-evidenced. None of them tests the others' position.

800 h

Ψ₀ — purity

One pre-cleaned egocentric source, no cross-embodiment pooling, no internet video. Beats baselines pretrained on more than 10× the data by over 40% absolute success. The cleaning pipeline is mostly the decision about what never enters.

versus
1,000,000 h

DYNA-2 — volume

Deliberately performs no visual or embodiment-specific processing to close the gap to the robot. Reports the first human-to-robot transfer scaling law, with an inflection between 10k and 100k hours where cross-embodiment knowledge appears to emerge from scale alone.

Between them sits EgoScale at 20,854 hours — the only work with a fitted curve (L = 0.024 − 0.003·ln(D), R² = 0.9983) and an explicit argument that noisy labels at scale are affordable if the action representation cancels the dominant noise mode. These three cannot all be the whole story, and no published experiment separates them.
