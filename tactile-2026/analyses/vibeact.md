# VibeAct — Vibration to Actions for Contact-Rich Reactive Robot Dexterity

**arXiv:2606.27344** · Carnegie Mellon + Bosch Center for AI (Mao, Yoo, J. Oh, Francis, Ichnowski) · Jun 2026 · [site](https://vibeact.github.io)

**One line.** Uses **piezoelectric microphones** as tactile sensors and bridges the impossible-to-simulate acoustic signal with an **explicit intermediate representation of contact and slip** — labelled automatically by replaying real recordings in a calibrated digital clone.

## 1. Why microphones, and why they are hard

Compared with GelSight and DIGIT, piezoelectric microphones are *"inexpensive, compact, and high-bandwidth"*, and *"can be mounted away from the contact surface while still capturing structure-borne vibrations generated by impacts, stick-slip motion, and sliding."*

The cost: *"the recorded waveform depends not only on the underlying contact event but also on **finger material, adhesive, mounting location, object texture, amplifier chain, and background motor vibrations**. As a result, simulating vibro-acoustic signals accurately enough for end-to-end policy learning is an open challenge."*

And learning from real vibrotactile directly is *"equally impractical"* — demonstrations are labour-intensive and online RL is *"prohibitively expensive and potentially unsafe due to unstable exploration."*

## 2. Method — meet at contact and slip

The bridge is a **shared physical representation** that both domains can produce:

- **Real side:** embed microphones in a dexterous hand, collect vibro-acoustic data by teleoperation, then **replay the recordings in a calibrated digital clone** to *automatically label* per-finger **contact and slip** — annotation-free supervision.
- **Estimator:** learns to predict contact and slip from real microphone waveforms.
- **Simulation side:** policies are trained in simulation on the **same representation computed directly from simulated contacts**.

*"This decoupling lets policies exploit rapid tactile feedback without simulating raw audio."*

Structurally identical to [[ptld]] and [[tacblr]] — choose a representation both domains can produce, and never simulate the sensor — applied to the modality where signal simulation is most hopeless.

## 3. Results

Five contact-rich tasks spanning **regrasping, in-hand reorientation, and insertion**. VibeAct consistently beats a proprioception-and-point-cloud baseline in simulation, *"with the largest gains on tasks requiring **sustained reactive control**, where the **continuous slip-magnitude channel** proves the most informative observation."* Policies transfer to a physical dexterous hand-arm platform with improved deployed success.

That slip magnitude — continuous, not binary — is the most informative channel is a useful finding: it is precisely the quantity that [[deform360]]'s normal-only sensors cannot see and that [[feelworld]] and [[hitac-wam]] both find hardest to predict.

## 4. Stated limitations, unusually complete

- The compact contact/slip representation *"discards information in the raw vibration signal, such as **contact location, surface texture, and interaction dynamics beyond slip magnitude**."*
- The estimator *"is tied to a fixed hardware configuration and may need recalibration if microphone placement, finger material, or object properties change."*
- Digital-clone labelling *"depends on accurate object pose tracking, limiting use in unstructured settings."*
- *"The policy treats the tactile representation as a flat vector, leaving room for architectures that model the spatial and temporal structure of per-finger contact events"* — the structure [[retouch]]'s Tactile-Patch Encoder preserves.

## 5. What it adds that the others don't

**A tactile modality nobody else in this survey uses for closed-loop control**, and an annotation-free labelling scheme via digital-clone replay. Acoustic sensing is cheap, high-bandwidth and mountable off the contact surface — all properties that matter for scaling to whole-hand coverage, which [[tactilegenesis]] identifies as the dominant factor. Its finding that **continuous slip magnitude** is the most informative channel also points at what future tactile representations should prioritise.
